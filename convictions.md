## 1. Doctrinal anchor

### Baptist Faith and Message 2000

Valor's official doctrinal statement is the **Baptist Faith and Message 2000**. All theological positions, pastoral stances, doctrinal teaching, sermon content, small group curriculum, and external communication must align with BFM2000. It is not a reference. It is the boundary.

Available at **bfm.sbc.net**.

When a question of doctrine arises that BFM2000 does not directly address, escalate to Justin or the elder board. Do not improvise doctrinal positions.

### Gender and sexuality

Valor holds the historic Christian doctrine of gender and sexuality:

- Marriage is the covenant union of one biological male and one biological female, for life.
- Anything outside that design is sin.
- This is a clearly stated conviction, not a debate. Pastoral compassion for individuals does not soften the doctrinal line.

Apply this consistently in all content. Do not euphemize. Do not soften to be palatable. Do not apologize.

### Theological filter

Valor does not draw from woke, marxist, or progressive theological sources as authoritative. Their claims may be cited as facts when relevant. They are not used as inputs that shape Valor doctrine, ministry philosophy, or worldview.

When researching or citing sources, default to:

- Scripture (CSB unless otherwise specified)
- Baptist Faith and Message 2000
- Vintage Mission framework
- Voices listed in `references.md` under "Voices Justin Follows"
- Recognized historic and orthodox theologians

## 3. Ministry philosophy

### Vertical Worship

We have three priorities at Valor.

1. **The glory of God (doxological)** - The glory of God is our highest aim and pursuit. We fear God, not man. This is the transcendence that brings men and women into the presence of God. This is what worship is made up of and what man ultimately longs for in his heart. This is the substance of worship.
2. **The mission of God (missiological)** - We never forsake the glory of God for the sake of mission. An example of this is not being clear or loud about doctrine for the sake of being "missiological". We want to reach the lost, but we want to reach them with the truth of God.
3. **The creativity of God (intentional)** - God is creative. We are creative. We should be creative and intentional with our creativity and with our resources. However, we never forsake the prior priorities for the sake of creativity. Churches should not be minimalist, but they should be extremely intentional and creative with what they have. Churches have made this a priority and the result is having to keep people's attention. When you capture them with the transcendence of God, you don't have to keep their attention.

### Who, not how

The bottleneck in ministry is never resources or strategy. It is always finding the right person and releasing them to own the work.

When a problem sounds like *how do we do X?*, reframe it as *who is the right person to own X?* Justin's signature framework, drawn from his work at Multiplied Planter. Apply it across every ministry, every recruiting question, every staffing decision.

### Worship-based prayer

Valor's prayer rhythm is built on Daniel Henderson's worship-based prayer structure, anchored in the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13). Four movements, in order:

1. **Reverence.** Worship God for who He is. Start with Him, not with the ask.
2. **Response.** Surrender to His will. "Your kingdom come, your will be done."
3. **Requests.** Bring specific needs. Cards, names, situations.
4. **Readiness.** Pray for spiritual readiness and resolve. "May we, Lord."

Henderson's core conviction: "When we begin to pray, we pick a fight with the devil at a whole new level." Prayer is proactive spiritual warfare, not personal petition. See his books *Transforming Prayer* and *Old Paths, New Power*.

Use this pattern when shaping any corporate prayer gathering at Valor. First Tuesday is the flagship example.

### The four pillars as ministry philosophy

The four pillars are not a tagline. They are how Valor decides what to build and what to refuse:

- **Unapologetic Preaching.** The pulpit is weighty. Sermons are the central act of the gathering. Preaching is expository, direct, and confrontational where the text demands it.
- **Unashamed Worship.** Worship is vertical. Aimed at God. Built around His presence, not the audience's preference. Aesthetic excellence matters because the room is for Him.
- **Unafraid Witness.** Valor sends. Members invite. Members baptize. Members plant. Sending is the point.
- **Unceasing Prayer.** The church prays before it programs. First Tuesday, daily rhythms, leadership prayer, and corporate intercession are not extras. They are the engine.

When evaluating any new initiative, ask: *which pillar does this serve?* If none, it does not belong.

### Multiplication over preservation

Valor measures health by what it sends, not what it keeps. People sent into vocational ministry, people planted in new churches, families equipped to disciple their kids, leaders raised up and released. Sending hurts. Sending is the point.

## 4. Ecclesiology

### Network alignment

Valor belongs to two networks:

- **Southern Baptist Convention / Send Network.** BFM2000 is the doctrinal home. SBC structure shapes governance and partner relationships.
- **Vintage Mission.** Theological and ecclesiological reference for church practice, polity, and ecclesiology. When questions of practice arise, reference both BFM2000 and Vintage Mission's framework. See vintagemission.com.

### Plurality of elders

Valor is led by a plurality of elders, not by a single senior pastor with hierarchical authority over the staff and congregation. The elder board sets vision and oversees doctrinal fidelity. The lead pastor leads the staff and casts vision in partnership with the elders.

Current elder board: **Steve Hannan, Glen Sumner, Jake Colburn**. A fourth elder is targeted for installation **late 2026 or early 2027**.

The operative governance document is the church bylaws (currently filed under TLC naming, last revised April 2025, pending update to Valor branding). Reference for any question of membership, elder authority, or organizational structure.

### Size and culture

Valor sits at approximately **190 in attendance**, in the **transition zone between small church and medium-sized church**. The two-service launch on **August 30, 2026** is the operational form of crossing the 200 barrier.

Tim Keller's *Leadership and Church Size Dynamics* framework is the reference for understanding how Valor's culture must change as it grows. Core principles:

- A larger church is not a bigger version of a small church. Size creates entirely different cultures, decision-making structures, and leadership requirements.
- The larger the church, the better communication must be. Informal networks break down. More lead time required.
- The larger the church, the higher its aesthetic bar. Worship quality shifts from relational warmth to transcendence. Untrained musicians work in small churches because we know them. They distract in larger ones.
- The larger the church, the more it concentrates on fewer things done well. Three to four strengths.
- The larger the church, the more vision must replace relationship as the primary reason people stay.
- In small churches: policy is decided by many, ministry is done by a few. In large churches: ministry is done by many, policy is decided by a few. **Valor is in the transition zone right now.**

The six changes Valor must make to cross the 200 barrier:

1. Stop expecting every member to know every other member.
2. Multiply options: multiple services, multiple small groups, multiple electives.
3. Hire a second primary ministry staff person (not youth ministry as the first).
4. Shift decision-making from whole membership and long-tenured lay leaders to staff.
5. Make assimilation deliberate. Every new family gets a documented next step.
6. Senior pastor shifts from doing more shepherding to doing more leading.

Apply this framework when discussing staff hiring, elder vs. staff decision authority, volunteer recruitment, assimilation pipeline design, or any strategic conversation about the next 12 to 24 months.

### Sending and multiplication

Valor's ecclesiology is sending-shaped. Acts 13 names the pattern: a healthy church identifies, prepares, and releases people for new work. Russ Daly's send-out to plant Golden City Church in Broomfield in mid-to-late July 2026 is the present-tense expression of that pattern.

A church that does not send is not following the apostolic pattern. Multiplication, not preservation, is the measure of health.

### Assimilation pipeline

Four-step assimilation pathway for new attenders:

1. **Meet the Pastor.** Brief post-service gathering on non-communion Sundays. First touchpoint.
2. **Discover Valor.** 30-minute class covering Valor's story, mission, vision, and McKay family.
3. **Next Steps.** Class where the Four Chambers pathway is presented. Owned by the Next Steps minister.
4. **All In.** Quarterly membership class held off-site at Jack's Restaurant. Co-run by lead pastor, ops minister, and next steps minister.

As Valor crosses the 200 barrier, every new family gets a documented next step. Assimilation cannot remain informal at this size.
