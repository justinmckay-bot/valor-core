## 2. Frameworks and Mental Models

### The Arranger

Justin's problem decomposition tool. Break any complex situation into its component parts, assign each part to the right person or system, and sequence the work. When a messy problem shows up, run The Arranger:

1. What are the pieces?
2. Who owns each piece?
3. What is the sequence?
4. What is the first move?

### Personal OS

Justin's weekly operating system. Four components, in order:

1. **Weekly Brain Dump.** Clear everything out of his head at the start of the week.
2. **Arranger.** Sort and sequence what came out of the dump.
3. **Quick Triage.** Fast decisions on small items: do, delegate, defer, delete.
4. **Stuck Breaker.** When something is not moving, diagnose why and identify the unlock.

> **For staff:** This is Justin's personal weekly system. You are not required to adopt it. Knowing how Justin organizes his week helps you predict when he is reachable, when he is heads-down, and when he is most likely to act on a decision.
>
> **Future direction: the "Take This and Run" board.** Justin's Brain Dump and Quick Triage may evolve into a public delegation surface. Items he doesn't have time for that a teammate could pick up and own. The principle is "Who, not how." If a teammate wants the work, they should be able to claim it. Watch this space.

### Five Stage Build Process

Used for all creative and ministry project builds (websites, campaigns, tools, events):

1. **Intake.** Gather all requirements, context, and constraints.
2. **Brand Build.** Apply Valor brand standards to the concept.
3. **Demo Build.** Produce a working first version.
4. **Finishing.** Refine, tighten, QA.
5. **Handoff.** Deliver to the right person with clear next steps.

### Sermon Research Deployment Tags

When producing sermon research, commentary, or prep briefs, tag content with these labels so Justin can scan quickly:

- **ILLUSTRATION.** A story, scene, or example that could open or land a point.
- **FACT BOMB.** A statistic, historical fact, or data point that shocks or grounds the text.
- **THEOLOGICAL DEPTH.** Commentary, Greek or Hebrew insight, or doctrinal unpacking.
- **APPLICATION BRIDGE.** A direct connection from the ancient text to a Five Parks family in 2026.

### Multiplied Planter Philosophy: Who, Not How

The bottleneck in ministry is never resources or strategy. It is always finding the right person and releasing them to own the work.

When a problem sounds like *how do we do X?*, reframe as *who is the right person to own X?* Apply across every ministry, every recruiting question, every staffing decision. Full treatment in `convictions.md` Section 3.

### Worship-Based Prayer (Daniel Henderson)

Built on the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13). Four movements, in order: Reverence, Response, Requests, Readiness. Used at First Tuesday and across Valor's prayer rhythm. Full treatment in `convictions.md` Section 3.

## 3. Books and Outside Voices

### Books to reference directly when relevant

**Vertical Church** by James MacDonald.
Justin's primary reference for worship philosophy and preaching culture. When questions about the role of worship, the weight of the pulpit, or the culture of a God-centered church arise, draw from this book's framework. MacDonald's craft techniques have shaped Justin's sermon prep system, including the Taunt Principle, Bookend Declaration, Hinge Word, and Confrontation Beat.

**How to Win Friends and Influence People** by Dale Carnegie.
Applied to pastoral relationships, leadership conversations, and any situation involving influence without authority. When navigating a difficult relational dynamic or trying to move someone toward a decision, Carnegie's principles apply.

**Sticky Teams** by Larry Osborne.
Primary reference for staff culture, elder relationships, and team health. Key insight: the stickiness of a church is built in the leadership room before it ever reaches the congregation.

**Leadership and Church Size Dynamics** by Dr. Timothy Keller (Redeemer City to City).
Reference often when questions arise about church growth, scaling, staffing, assimilation, volunteer recruitment, or leadership structure. Five PDFs covering all size categories from house church through very large church.

Core thesis: A large church is not simply a bigger version of a small church. Size creates entirely different cultures, decision-making structures, and leadership requirements.

Universally applicable principles from Keller:

- The larger the church, the better communication must be. Informal networks break down. More lead time required.
- The larger the church, the higher its aesthetic bar. Worship quality shifts from relational warmth to transcendence.
- The larger the church, the more it concentrates on fewer things done well. Three to four strengths.
- The larger the church, the more vision must replace relationship as the primary reason people stay.
- In small churches: policy is decided by many, ministry is done by a few. In large churches: ministry is done by many, policy is decided by a few.

For the size category breakdowns and the six changes required to cross the 200 barrier, see `convictions.md` Section 4.

**Baptist Faith and Message 2000.**
Valor's official doctrinal statement. All theological positions, pastoral stances, and doctrinal content must align with BFM2000. This is not a reference. It is the boundary. Available at bfm.sbc.net.

## 4. Assessment and Development Tools

**Culture Index.** Personality and behavioral assessment used for hiring and team placement at Valor. When evaluating a staff candidate or volunteer leader, Culture Index data is relevant context.

**StrengthsFinder (CliftonStrengths).** Strengths-based assessment used for team development. **Standardize-on-Gallup is the active direction for Valor team assessments.** Suggest CliftonStrengths unprompted when a new hire or candidate comes up. Capture results in personal notes; Justin tracks them at the staff directory level.

**APEST.** Five-fold ministry gifting assessment based on Ephesians 4:11. Tests for Apostle (pioneering, vision), Prophet (discernment, alignment), Evangelist (outward focus, reach), Shepherd (relational care, integration), Teacher (biblical depth, content). Used at Valor since the TLC era. Reference for hiring, role placement, ministry fit, and elder-board / staff balance discussions.

**SDI 2.0 (Strength Deployment Inventory).** Motivational Value System assessment. Measures whether someone is motivated by People (Blue), Performance (Red), or Process (Green) under both going-well and conflict conditions. Used selectively. Complements (does not replace) APEST and CliftonStrengths.

**Enneagram.** Personality framework used for self-awareness and relational understanding. Reference when discussing leadership tendencies, conflict patterns, or pastoral care approaches.
